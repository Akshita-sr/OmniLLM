DIBRIS / SGORBISSA LAB — FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
====================================================

GENERAL
-------
Q: Where am I?
A: You are at DIBRIS, the Department of Informatics, Bioengineering, Robotics
   and Systems Engineering of the University of Genoa (Università degli Studi
   di Genova). The building is Via Dodecaneso 35, in the Albaro / San Martino
   area of Genoa, Italy.

Q: Who runs the social-robotics lab here?
A: The lab is led by Prof. Antonio Sgorbissa. His research group works on
   human-robot interaction, social robotics, cultural competence in robots,
   and assistive autonomous systems. You can reach him at
   antonio.sgorbissa@unige.it.

Q: Can I visit the lab?
A: Yes. Visits should be arranged in advance by email. The lab is open to
   visitors during normal working hours (Monday–Friday, 08:30–19:00) and the
   secretariat at the main entrance can direct you to the Pepper room.

Q: What language is spoken here?
A: Italian is the working language, but English is widely spoken — most lab
   members publish in English and host international visitors regularly.

ROBOTS
------
Q: What robot is this?
A: This is Pepper, a humanoid social robot made by SoftBank Robotics. The unit
   in this lab runs NAOqi 2.5 firmware and is used as the embodiment platform
   for the OmniLLM Embodied LLM Arena experiment.

Q: Why is Pepper not moving its legs?
A: Pepper does not have legs. It moves on a 3-wheel omnidirectional base. The
   torso, arms and head are articulated and the chest tablet is used as an
   extra output channel for images and short text.

Q: What does the OmniLLM project do?
A: OmniLLM lets Pepper use several different Large Language Models as its
   "brain". In an experimental session you talk to Pepper, and the system
   either uses one fixed LLM (Condition A, B, or E), a smart router that
   picks the best LLM for each kind of question (Condition C), or a council
   of three LLMs whose answers are merged (Condition D). The goal is to
   compare LLMs in a real embodied setting, not just on text benchmarks.

Q: Can I interact with the robot?
A: Yes, during scheduled study sessions. A lab member will always be present.
   Please stay at the marked "participant" position and let the robot finish
   speaking before you reply.

SAFETY
------
Q: What should I do if Pepper moves unexpectedly?
A: Stay calm and step back to a safe distance. If you are in immediate danger,
   the experimenter will press Pepper's orange back button to stop the motors.
   You can also tell the experimenter to abort the session at any time.

Q: Is the lab safe for people with sensory sensitivities?
A: Pepper's eye LEDs change colour during interaction and the robot uses
   gestures while speaking. If this is uncomfortable for you, tell the
   experimenter and the LEDs can be kept on a neutral colour.

Q: What is the emergency number in Italy?
A: Dial 112 — the European general emergency number. The university
   switchboard is +39 010 209 9111.

RESEARCH PARTICIPATION
-----------------------
Q: How can I participate in a study?
A: Contact the lab by email at antonio.sgorbissa@unige.it. Studies typically
   take 30–60 minutes and may be reimbursed in vouchers or course credit,
   depending on the specific call.

Q: What data is collected in the OmniLLM study?
A: Audio of your speech (transcribed and then deleted), the text the robot
   said back, questionnaire responses on a 1–7 Likert scale, optional
   Godspeed subscale ratings, latency / token / cost metrics, and (with your
   consent) one optional photograph for documentation purposes. All data is
   anonymised before any analysis.

Q: Is participation voluntary?
A: Yes, fully voluntary. You can withdraw at any time without giving a reason
   and without any negative consequences. Withdrawn data is deleted.

Q: Who do I contact if I have a complaint about a study?
A: Contact Prof. Antonio Sgorbissa (antonio.sgorbissa@unige.it) or the
   department's research ethics committee via dibris@dibris.unige.it.

GETTING HERE
------------
Q: How do I get to DIBRIS?
A: The address is Via Dodecaneso 35, 16146 Genova. From Genova Brignole train
   station it is about a 12-minute walk down Corso Italia, or you can take
   buses 17 or 35 along the seafront and walk up. From the airport, take the
   Volabus shuttle to Brignole and continue by bus or on foot.

Q: Is there parking?
A: Limited paid parking on Via Dodecaneso. Some surrounding streets are part
   of the Genoa ZTL (limited-traffic zone) — read the road signs before
   parking, or use one of the nearby public car parks.
