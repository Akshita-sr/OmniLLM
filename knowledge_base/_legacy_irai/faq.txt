IRAI LAB — FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS (FAQ)
============================================

GENERAL
-------
Q: What does IRAI stand for?
A: Intelligent Robotics and Artificial Intelligence. The IRAI Lab was established
   in 2021 as part of the university's commitment to AI and robotics research.

Q: Who runs the lab?
A: The lab is led by Prof. Alejandro Martinez (Principal Investigator) and
   Dr. Sarah Patel (Lab Manager and co-investigator). Contact the lab at
   irai-lab@university.edu.

Q: Can I visit the lab?
A: Yes! Visitors are welcome during opening hours (Mon–Fri 08:00–20:00,
   Sat 09:00–14:00). Please contact us in advance to arrange a visit.
   For participant visits (user studies), you will receive a specific appointment.

ROBOTS
------
Q: What robots does the lab have?
A: The lab has four robotic platforms: Pepper (social humanoid robot, SoftBank
   Robotics), NAO (smaller humanoid, also SoftBank), Buddy (companion robot,
   Blue Frog Robotics), and TurtleBot4 (mobile navigation research platform).

Q: Can I interact with the robots?
A: Yes, during open lab days and scheduled user studies. Always have a lab member
   present and maintain a 1-metre safety perimeter around moving robots.

Q: What is Pepper used for in the lab?
A: Pepper is our primary human-robot interaction (HRI) research platform.
   Currently it powers the OmniLLM Embodied LLM Arena study, comparing
   different LLM backends for social interaction tasks.

SAFETY
------
Q: What should I do in an emergency?
A: Emergency number (internal): 999. External: +44 20 7946 0999.
   Emergency exits are signposted — Staircase A (near lifts) is the primary exit.
   The assembly point is the car park behind Building C.

Q: Where is the first aid kit?
A: Room 310 (breakout/café area), mounted on the wall near the coffee machine.

Q: Are there any safety rules I need to know?
A: The main rules are: (1) Announce yourself when entering — robots may be active.
   (2) Keep 1 metre away from moving robots. (3) Do not touch equipment unless
   invited by a lab member. (4) No food or drink near robot equipment.

RESEARCH PARTICIPATION
-----------------------
Q: How can I participate in a user study?
A: Contact Alice Chen (a.chen@university.edu) or sign up via the participant
   recruitment form on the lab website. Studies typically take 30–60 minutes
   and may offer a small payment or course credit.

Q: What data is collected in the OmniLLM study?
A: Voice recordings (transcribed and then deleted), questionnaire responses,
   interaction timestamps, and performance metrics. No personally identifiable
   information is stored after anonymisation. Full ethics approval obtained.

Q: Is participation voluntary?
A: Yes, fully voluntary. You may withdraw at any time without giving a reason,
   with no negative consequences.
